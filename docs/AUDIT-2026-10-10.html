Where GenX loses customers, and what to build first

Every finding below was read out of the repository at G:\Zuoyi stitchpro\genxdigitizing. Nothing here is inferred from a template or assumed from a similar business. Where the brief and the code disagree, the code is reported and the disagreement is named.

01 Critical problems

These are not improvements. Each one is a working path in production that fails, silently or otherwise.

An upload-wizard lead converts to an order with no artwork attached

Fixed

The upload wizard is the main conversion path. It recorded files in one text format; convert-to-order read a different one. The reader matched nothing, its attach block was skipped entirely, and the order was created without the customer's file. Staff saw “Order created!” and the designer opened an empty order.

The same reader used non-global matching, so even on the paths that did work only the first file of a multi-file upload was ever attached.

Why it matters: the job stalls at the exact moment the customer has already committed a file and their details.

Every order that uses a coupon fails

Fixed

orders/create wrote coupon_code, coupon_id and discount_amount onto the order row. Those columns have never existed — 001_initial_schema.sql:114-140, and no later migration adds them. PostgREST rejects the whole insert. The missing-column retry only strips idempotency_key, so the coupon keys survive it and the retry fails too.

The route also never recorded the redemption, so the usage counter never incremented and the admin coupon report undercounted.

Why it matters: a customer arriving through a coupon — the most expensive kind to acquire — sees “Could not create the order” and leaves.

Nothing on a schedule ever looks at a lead

Open

crm_leads.follow_up_at exists as a column and is written by nothing and read by nothing except an AI prompt. The only scheduled work in the whole system is /api/cron/sla-check every 30 minutes and /api/cron/subscription-renewal daily — both of which look at orders and subscriptions, never at leads. There is no pg_cron anywhere.

A lead that goes quiet is therefore never surfaced to anyone. “Follow up in three days” is a human remembering, and when the team is busy, they don't.

Why it matters: this is the single largest revenue leak in the repository, and the brief's own rule — never lose a customer to a missed follow-up — is the one thing the system cannot currently do at all.

The lead timeline is free text, and the board overwrites it

Fixed

There is no lead-events table. The entire history of a lead lives in one notes text column, and five different writers append to it with read-modify-write and no atomic append.

The leads board built its update from the copy loaded at page render, so any event appended server-side while the board sat open was destroyed by the next drag of a stage.

Fixed by an append-only event log. Migration 052 adds lead_events: one row per thing that happened, with the type, the actor, the from/to stage, and a summary. Nothing updates or deletes a row — RLS allows insert and select only, so append-only is enforced rather than promised. Every writer now records an event: both creation paths, the email route (success and failure), the chat reply, order conversion, and the board's own stage drags and manual adds.

The board renders the timeline from events, falling back to parsing the notes column for leads that predate the table — no back-fill, because a line of text cannot be decomposed into actor and stage without inventing them, and invented history in an audit trail is worse than absent history.

Why it matters: the record of who spoke to a customer, and when, is the business's memory. Today it can be erased by a mouse drag.

Migration 032 cannot commit, so its policies never apply

Fixed

032_enable_rls_core_tables.sql:37-38 created policies on messages.sender_id and messages.recipient_id. Those columns do not exist — the real ones are from_user and to_user (001:190-191). The file runs as one transaction, so it aborted and none of its policies — users, leads, audit logs, subscribers — were ever created. Lines 26-27 also collided with policy names from 001.

Rewritten to do only what its name claims. Two of its policies were worth losing: the users_read_all one would have let every signed-in user read every user row including email and role, and the rest duplicated 001 with the right columns already.

Why it mattered: a fresh environment could not be built at all, and anyone auditing RLS by reading the migrations would believe protections were in place that were not.

Two tables the code depends on are created by no file in the repository

Verified live

subscribers (written by app/api/subscribe/route.ts:26) and blog_comments (used by two routes) had RLS policies and application code but no CREATE TABLE anywhere. Migration 042 says so explicitly about blog_comments and nobody acted on it.

Both are now created by migration 051, matching the columns the routes actually use. The comment table keeps 042's posture: no anonymous read policy, so author_email cannot leak through the public endpoint.

Applied and verified on 2026-10-10. Both tables now answer on the live database where they previously returned 404 PGRST205, and an anonymous write to either is refused with 42501.

Failure logs are write-only, and bounces are recorded then ignored

Open

notification_failures and email_failures are written on failure and read only by lib/invariants.ts, which is called solely from /api/admin/health — an endpoint on no cron, linked from no screen. resolved is never set by any code. There is no retry anywhere.

Bounce and complaint webhooks are signature-verified and working — and land in email_bounces / email_complaints, which nothing reads. No suppression list, no flag on the lead, no alert.

Why it matters: the team keeps emailing dead addresses and complaining customers, and enough of that gets the sending domain suspended — which stops all customer email at once.

02 UX problems

The brief asks for Upload Design as the primary CTA and WhatsApp as secondary. On the device most of your traffic uses, neither is true.

The mobile sticky CTA bar never renders at any screen width

Fixed

The bar holding WhatsApp, Register and “Upload Design” is sm:hidden and lives inside HeroSection, which is rendered inside <div className="hidden md:block">. Below 640px the ancestor is hidden; at 640px and above the bar hides itself. There is no width where both pass.

Because that bar was believed to be there, the WhatsApp floating button is separately hidden on the mobile homepage — the comment says it would conflict with the bar. So on a phone at / there is no sticky CTA, no WhatsApp button, and no chat widget.

Why it matters: the mobile homepage has no persistent way to start an order.

The hero asks for an account, not a design

Fixed

On mobile, the hero that actually renders offers “Sign Up / Login” as its primary action, styled as a faint translucent button, with WhatsApp beside it. The accessible reduced-motion variant of the same hero links to /upload instead — so the fallback out-converts the default.

On desktop the prominent gradient button is likewise “Sign In / Register”; the only Upload action is a scroll-triggered pill that fades in after the hero.

Across the page body, seven separate CTAs point at /contact and the closing CTA points at /register.

A guest is shown a reference number that exists nowhere

Fixed

Fixed: the reference is now generated on the server (lib/lead-reference.ts), written into the lead's notes, returned to the browser and shown on the confirmation screen. Its alphabet omits 0/O/1/I/L so it survives being read down a phone, and the customer is told plainly to quote it.

Still open: there is no self-service status lookup, and registering still does not attach a guest's submitted job to their new account — so the confirmation screen's "Create Account to Track Order" button promised something the product does not do. It now reads "Create an account" and sits below WhatsApp until the linking exists.

Fields the customer fills in, confirms, and the server never receives

Fixed

Fixed for stitch count and Special Instructions. Both were collected, both appeared on the confirmation screen the customer approved, and neither was in the request body — the wizard simply dropped them. Stitch count now reaches orders.stitch_count; because the customer types into a free-text box, only a clean number is written to the integer column and anything else ("approx 8k") is kept as text in the placement notes rather than mis-parsed. Instructions are appended to the placement notes so the digitizer reads them.

On the guest path, garment, fabric and per-file output format were appended by the client and ignored by the route: the labels survived only as raw option ids pasted into a text blob, and a guest who switched file 2 to PES had that choice dropped without warning. Fixed — the route reads all three, stores the labels the customer actually saw, and writes each file's requested output format on its own line.

Why it matters: the customer believes they told you, and the digitizer never sees it.

Uploads have no timeout, so a stalled connection spins forever

Fixed

Fixed on all five upload paths — guest upload, the two logged-in order uploaders, designer output upload, and chat attachments — each now sets a three-minute deadline and reports a timeout in words. Previously a stalled connection never settled the promise on any of them: the progress bar simply stopped. The order paths say what to do about it, because the order already exists at that point.

/about shows four invented team members with fabricated histories

Fixed

app/(marketing)/about/AboutContent.tsx:185-214 renders four people with initials for surnames and specific tenure claims — “8+ years in commercial embroidery digitizing”, “Former screen-print designer”, “5+ years running multi-head commercial embroidery machines”. No data source backs any of them. This is the same defect class as the testimonials already removed, and it is live.

Why it matters: fabricated credentials on a page selling expertise is deceptive advertising exposure, not a copy nit.

A “Guaranteed within 12 Hours” claim that the terms disclaim

Fixed

The conversion page promises a guarantee; terms-and-conditions:72 says turnaround targets “are not guaranteed”. The urgent tier is also given three incompatible figures across the site — 2–4 hours, 3 hours, and 6 hours.

Why it matters: a guarantee the contract disclaims is the setup for a chargeback.

Resolved, 2026-10-10. The wider problem underneath was that the site said "12-hour turnaround" in about twenty places while lib/sla.ts schedules standard at 24 hours — and the order confirmation email said "12–24h" on top of that. Three numbers for one thing.

The published claim is now the range the tiers actually cover: 3–24h, from a single TURNAROUND_RANGE_LABEL constant, with a test asserting its endpoints are real tiers and cannot drift from TURNAROUND_HOURS. Per-tier copy now says 24 / 6 / 3. Large and complex work keeps its "~12h", which is correct — BIG_DESIGN_HOURS is deliberately 12.

Eight invented client logos and a fabricated-scarcity banner are loaded guns

Fixed

components/marketing/ClientLogoWall.tsx lists eight named companies under “Trusted by Embroidery Businesses Worldwide”. It is currently imported nowhere, so it is not live — but the next person building a logo section will wire it up. The OfferBanner “Order in next 2 hours” scarcity copy is likewise unreachable today and has no real queue signal behind it. Both should be deleted, not left parked.

03 Business and order-flow problems

A quote cannot be recorded, and one bad stage value blanks the board

Fixed

The database allows quote_sent and negotiation; the leads UI offered only New, Contacted, Won and Lost. So no member of staff could record that a quote had gone out — which means quote-to-order conversion cannot be measured and leads awaiting a quote cannot be found.

Worse, the card lookup used a non-null assertion. Any row holding an unlisted stage threw and took the whole leads board down for every user.

Staff can create a $0 order from a typo

Fixed

The conversion price was checked only for truthiness, and the string "0" is truthy. It was then written straight to the order with no comparison to the tier price and flowed into the invoice, the customer email and revenue reporting.

A converted customer could get an account nobody can access, silently

Fixed

Converting a lead with no existing user creates an auth account and emails a set-password link. That email was fired with a bare .catch that logged to the console. If it failed, the account existed, the customer never knew, and no one was told.

Why it matters: a paying customer who cannot reach their own order is a refund conversation.

A failed artwork upload discarded the whole enquiry

Fixed

On the contact form, the storage upload sat inside the handler's try/catch and throws on failure. A bucket hiccup turned “customer told us what they need” into a 500 with no lead row written at all.

Partial upload failures were reported as total failure

Fixed

Both the guest route and the logged-in wizard rejected oversized files mid-loop, after earlier files had already been stored. The customer was then told their artwork was missing when some of it was on the order.

The CRM edits an active-state field that does not exist on that table

Fixed

ContactsUI.tsx:64 updated clients.is_active. The clients table has no such column — the real one is on users, which the admin clients screen correctly uses. The write failed, and because the badge and the button label read the same phantom field they were wrong too: the button always offered to deactivate. It now reads and writes users.is_active, so CRM and Admin agree on one customer.

Still open, and a decision for you: service_tiers.credit_cost is read in four places and exists in no migration. Reads return undefined and fall back to plan defaults, so a per-tier credit price set in the database does nothing. Adding the column would make the code work as written, but it sets what a design costs in credits — a pricing decision, not a bug fix, so it is left alone.

The change-log table does not exist, so staff are never told about order edits

Fixed

order_edit_log is created only by supabase/setup/step6 — a manual setup script, not a migration. Verified against the live database on 2026-10-10: the table is absent. Three code paths depend on it.

Customers can still change their order spec — the order update succeeds — but the log insert fails, and app/api/orders/[id]/edit/route.ts:134 only notifies the team when the log row was written. So today a customer edits their design brief and nobody is told. The order proceeds on the old spec.

The admin orders screen and the sidebar badge also query it; both degrade to zero rather than crashing, which is why this stayed invisible.

Fixed and verified live. Migration 051 creates the table. Confirmed against the production database on 2026-10-10: the table exists, an anonymous insert is refused with 42501 (row-level security), and the service-role path reaches it — so the edit notification to admins will now fire instead of being suppressed by the failed log write.

Any client could write change-log rows against any other customer's order

Fixed

order_edit_log exists only in the setup scripts, not in any migration, so a database built from migrations alone does not have it — while two code paths write to it. Its insert policy checked changed_by = auth.uid() and nothing else. A client editing their own order supplies their own id and the order id, so they could log changes against any order in the system, and staff review those rows on the admin orders screen.

The table is now created by migration 051 and the insert policy also requires the order to belong to the caller, mirroring the read policy beside it.

The AI sales agent will invent policy, and its drafts leave no trace

Fixed

Pricing is properly grounded — a tool reads live service_tiers and the prompt forbids memory prices. Policy is not. Refund, free-sample, guarantee and deadline questions have no tool behind them, while the prompt instructs the model to “state the actual sample policy”. It will invent one.

Fixed — policy is now grounded. lib/ai/policy.ts holds every policy the assistant may state, each entry naming where the business already publishes it, with tests asserting the turnaround sentence cannot drift from lib/sla.ts or the price floor from SITE_CLAIMS. A get_business_policy tool serves it, and the prompt now says that list is the only thing it may state as policy — anything outside it gets "I'll check with the team".

Fixed — drafts are recorded. Migration 053 adds ai_drafts: the draft, the exact briefing it was written from, the staff member, the model, token usage, and an outcome a human sets. Admin/crm only, no delete policy. Human approval remains mandatory; nothing sends.

Fixed — the CRM notes are no longer fed in as fact. They arrive labelled "internal, written by staff, may be stale or wrong", with this system's own machine-written lines stripped first — a note about a failed upload is not something the customer said.

Correction to an earlier version of this report. claude-opus-5 (lib/ai/sales-agent.ts:44) is a valid model id — this was reported as a likely failure and it is not. The default has been moved up to claude-opus-5-5, the current Opus and cheaper per token, with SALES_AGENT_MODEL still able to pin either one. The real gaps here are the two above: no draft is persisted, and policy answers have no data behind them.

Why it matters: auto-send is correctly disabled, so no invented policy reaches a customer yet. The grounding gap and the model id are what stand between this and a safe draft mode.

04 Security problems

The pattern behind nearly all of these is the same: middleware required only some authenticated role on a path, and the route trusted identifiers from the request body. The fixes below move each check into the route, where the caller's own identity is available.

Any registered account could confirm any other account's email

Fixed

/api/auth/auto-confirm accepted an arbitrary userId or email and confirmed it with the service-role key. Its paginated email-search branch doubled as a user-enumeration oracle. It now confirms only the caller's own address.

The chat notify endpoint let anyone push forged notifications to anyone

Fixed

/api/message-notify took the recipient and the message body from the request. Any logged-in account could send attacker-authored text — “Payment confirmed, open /client/invoices” — to any user id, admins included, and could flood an inbox. It now accepts only a message id, derives everything from that row, and requires the caller to be its sender.

The welcome-email route was an open relay for branded mail

Fixed

Any free account could use the company's Resend reputation to send branded mail to arbitrary recipients. It now sends only to the caller's own address.

A missing webhook secret silently made the inbound-email endpoint public

Fixed

Signature verification returned true when RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET was unset — so anyone could POST forged email.received payloads that run with the service-role key and notify every admin. It now fails closed in production.

Login used to advance a lead to Won and wipe its notes

Fixed

The auth callback auto-moved any matching lead to won on login, and did it by selecting only id and then writing lead.notes + note — so the concatenation destroyed every existing note on the lead. Logging in is not a sale.

Push subscription keys have no RLS at all

Fixed

002_push_subscriptions.sql:2-13 created user_push_subscriptions with indexes and no RLS and no policies, in no later migration either. Every user's endpoint, p256dh and auth keys were readable and writable by anon and authenticated. Possession of those three values is enough to send push notifications to that device.

Migration 051 adds owner-only policies for select, insert, update and delete.

Correction, verified 2026-10-10 against the live database. This was reported as "no RLS at all, anon can read and write every row". The table does have 17 rows, but an anonymous read returns Content-Range: */0 — RLS is enabled. Supabase auto-enables RLS on new tables in the public schema, which is the platform behaviour migration 042 already refers to. So the exposure was never open. What is actually missing is policies: with RLS on and none defined, no client key can touch the table at all, and the app only works because every path uses the service-role client. 051 makes that explicit rather than incidental. Applied and verified on 2026-10-10: the service role sees all 17 rows, an anonymous read sees none, and an anonymous write is refused with 42501.

Loose policies on audit logs, users and inbound email

Fixed

audit_logs insert was WITH CHECK (true) for any authenticated user (001:515) — the audit trail could be written by anyone. The route also took user_id from the request body, so an actor could be recorded as somebody else.

The blanket policy is replaced by a scoped one, and the actor now comes from the session. 033:49's anon insert on received_emails is dropped — the only writer is the service-role webhook. The over-broad users_read_all that 032 would have added no longer exists.

07 Recommended implementation order

The brief's phase list is sound. This is the same order, with the specific work that belongs in each phase and the reason it sits there.

  1. Make the paths that already exist actually work

    The fixes marked done in this document, plus the remaining P0s: push-subscription RLS, the two missing tables, migration 032, and the clients.is_active mismatch.

    Why first: every one of these is a live path failing today. No new feature matters while ordering, artwork and conversion are unreliable.

  2. Give the lead a real history

    An append-only lead-events table with actor, type and timestamp. Migrate the existing notes entries into it, then write events instead of appending text.

    Why second: the follow-up engine, the attention dashboard and every funnel metric read from this. Building them on the text column means building them twice.

  3. Follow-up engine

    Due dates that are set when a lead is created or contacted, one cron that finds what is due, a queue screen, and logged idempotent sends with a cancel path.

    Why third: this is the brief's central rule, and the events table from step 2 is what makes a follow-up auditable rather than another text append.

  4. Needs-attention dashboard

    Replace the admin home's passive totals with clickable counts: unanswered leads, quotes awaiting reply, orders approaching SLA, notifications that failed to deliver.

    Why fourth: cheap once step 2 exists, and it is what makes the follow-up engine actually get used by a busy team.

  5. Fix the customer-facing funnel

    Restore the mobile CTA, make Upload Design the hero action on both breakpoints, persist and show a real reference, add a guest status lookup, and reconcile the turnaround claims with the terms. Send the fields the wizard already collects.

    Why fifth, not first: more traffic into a funnel that still loses artwork and coupons wastes the traffic. This work is high value and should follow the reliability fixes, not precede them.

  6. Trust cleanup

    Delete the invented team bios, the client logo wall and the scarcity banner; replace the absolute security claims; unify turnaround wording.

    Why here: quick, and it should not wait behind engineering — but it is not what unblocks revenue.

  7. AI safety, then channels

    Persist and audit drafts, ground policy answers in data, fix the model id. Only then the WhatsApp webhook and the unified conversation identity that Instagram, Facebook and email will share.

    Why last: all three of these are new surface. They are safe to build once the record they write into — contacts, conversations, events — is trustworthy.
